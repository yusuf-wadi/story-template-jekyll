## Historical Context

The idea of a conscious model of reality is not new. In fact, it’s as old as human thought itself. The ancient Greeks pondered the nature of reality through their concept of the Logos, or the divine reason that gives order to the universe. Islamic scholars like Al-Ghazali spoke of the continuous creation of reality by Allah, where each moment is freshly created by divine will. The Vedic traditions of India conceived of Maya, or the illusion of separateness from the divine consciousness.

What’s fascinating is how these ancient insights align with our modern understanding. When quantum physicists discovered that observation affects reality at the subatomic level, they were stumbling upon what mystics had intuited for millennia. The observer effect isn’t just some quirk of quantum mechanics; it is in fact a fundamental hint about the nature of reality itself.

## Intro

The idea at its most essential is the assertion that the soul (self) interacts with reality (non-self), and its main function is as observer and decision maker. **Observation** and **choice **flow within one another to result in the self’s perceived reality. ### Choice != Control

Choosing and aligning oneself towards a discernment is not the same thing as causing, willing, or controlling that thing.

Example: you can choose you want to buy a coffee but there are more factors than you can count that control whether or not you can actually get a coffee. And those factors are beyond you, and even beyond anyone’s control. Perhaps you become paralyzed, perhaps you fall in a coma, or your car stops working, or whatever the case may be. You think when you choose something you can control the outcome but you can not. You can only hope that it can be so.

### Self Control is a Myth

To say there is such thing as self control is to say that your will over yourself is supreme above all else.  
There is only one Supreme Will, and that is of the original Unobserved Observer, Almighty **God**.

### **God**

If you confirm with certainty that you exist, you as a reasonable being should then be able to observe with the natural faculties allotted that there were things before you. Who determined those things to be so? If you say it was “nothing”, than what is “nothing” besides that which is not like another thing in any way shape or form. Can you have multiple “nothings”? It is futile to attempt to comprehend a thing like no other thing, but to comprehend what is around you is a different task.

A reasoning task, one that we as a species are exceptional at.

We can reason that we exist, and that things existed before us. We reason that these things are in a certain way, when they could have been in any other way, but they are not. We observe that things are moving away from each other, so we reason that they were together. We observe a background radiating energy, and we reason that it is from an event left over.

We observe and reason all of these things to come to one conclusion.

We are because it was decided that we will be. Simple as that.

Could an unobserving, unthinking, and unfeeling nothing cause something that will feel, think, and observe?

Does a child give birth to it’s mother? Can a rock spontaneously become a skyscraper or an iPhone? It’s an air of arrogance that envelops us if we believe that we are conscious but what we came from is not.

The apophatic view of God, describing God as the ineffable, immutable, incomprehensible, inconceivable, etc.. It is more useful to understand what God is not, rather than approaching the edge of reality to take a peak at what God is or might be, and risk falling.

### You

Are finite. Limited. Small in scope, but great in purpose. You are the fractal incarnation of the Will of God. You are not your flesh, or your brain, or some region of your brain. These things you observe are merely perfect representations of God’s ultimate will made manifest.

Had God willed you to be illiterate, your faculties of reading would simply cease. What must be understood about this though, is that given the perfection of God’s Will, and His immutable and ineffable nature, the result of your spontaneous illiteracy would be traceable and not discontinuous in any way. All paths would have lead to what you observed in reality.

We as humans attempt to replicate this perfection in our lives, we call this planning. In order for things to happen, it must follow logical next steps in order to see fruition.

If an author writes a story, the readers would spot right away when something is shoehorned in for the plot, or some discontinuous leap in logic is made in order to tie some loose end.

God, obviously, is the best story teller. The best of planners.

### Reality

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/499/1*KjU6CVpjWPf2UDYVXp8OKQ.jpeg)

_the universal network_

We are in the mind of God. God’s self reference.

Reality is not a thing separate from God, but rather the manifestation of His consciousness. When we perceive reality, we are perceiving a fraction of God’s thought made tangible. The mountains, the oceans, the stars: not merely creations but expressions of divine contemplation.

Consider how your own thoughts work. When you imagine something vividly, it exists in your mind with a certain reality. Now imagine a mind infinitely more powerful, where thoughts don’t just exist as internal images but manifest as existence itself.

That is reality as God’s self-reference.

The laws of physics, the constants of nature, the unfolding of time, these are not arbitrary rules but the logical consistency of God’s thought. Just as your own thoughts follow patterns and rules that make sense to you, reality follows the perfect logic of divine reason.

What we call “natural laws” are simply the grammar of God’s language of creation. The universe expands because God’s thought expands. Entropy increases because divine consciousness flows in a particular direction. Causality exists because God’s thoughts are coherent and sequential.

When you observe reality, you are not merely seeing objects. You are witnessing God’s ongoing self-contemplation. Your consciousness, limited as it is by the diamond window of perception, is nonetheless granted the profound privilege of participating in this divine self-reference.

This is why reality feels both external to us and intimately connected to our consciousness. It is external because it is primarily God’s thought, not ours. Yet it feels connected because our consciousness is derived from and participates in the divine consciousness.

> “We will show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth.” (Surah Fussilat 41:53).

‘The signs in the horizons’ as the external reality, and ‘the signs within ourselves’ as our consciousness, two aspects of the same divine reality.

In this model, the question is not whether reality exists independently of our observation. Rather, the question is: whose observation ultimately matters? And the answer is clear: reality exists within God’s observation first and foremost. Our observation is secondary, derivative, a gift granted to us by the primary Observer.

## Personal Reflections

I remember the first time I truly grasped the limitation of my own will. I had planned meticulously for a project, accounted for every variable I could think of, and was certain of success. Then, in an instant, circumstances beyond my control rendered the entire endeavor impossible. My initial reaction was frustration and even anger. But in that moment of powerlessness came clarity.

What if my perception of control was the true illusion? What if the very concept of “my will” versus “God’s will” was a false dichotomy? Perhaps what I call “my will” is simply the localized expression of the divine will as it manifests through this particular node of consciousness that I call “me.”

> “But you cannot will unless Allah wills.” (Surah Al-Insan 76:30).

This isn’t a negation of human will but rather a profound insight into its true nature. Your will exists, but it exists within the ocean of God’s will, like a wave exists within the ocean itself.

## Critiques

Some might argue that this model removes human responsibility. If all is within God’s will, why should I be held accountable for my actions? This misunderstands the nature of will and choice. Your choices remain yours, even as they exist within the greater reality of God’s will. The diamond window of perception grants you a genuine, if limited, view of reality. The choices you make through that window are authentic expressions of your soul.

Others might claim this model is unfalsifiable and therefore meaningless from a scientific perspective. But this criticism misses the point. Not all meaningful truths about existence are amenable to scientific falsification. The very consciousness through which we perceive reality and do science cannot itself be reduced to scientific observation, for it is the ground of all observation.

## Future

As we develop more sophisticated artificial intelligence, we will inevitably confront questions about consciousness, will, and reality that this model addresses. Can a machine observe reality in the way humans do? Can it make choices in the meaningful sense we’ve discussed? Or will it merely simulate these processes without the inner light of consciousness?

The answers to these questions will not just determine the future of technology but will force us to reckon with what it means to be conscious observers in this reality. Perhaps in our quest to create artificial minds, we will come to better understand the divine mind within which our own consciousness resides.

The fractal nature of reality suggests that each level of existence reflects the whole. Just as a hologram contains the entire image in each fragment, perhaps each conscious being contains a reflection of the divine consciousness. In understanding ourselves, we may glimpse something of the ineffable nature of God. Not by looking outward to the edge of reality, but by looking inward to the source of our own awareness.
