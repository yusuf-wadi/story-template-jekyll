The written stretches far beyond simply being the human word placed on a line. This concept is one that we as Muslims should comprehend, especially when approaching the idea of the written fate (Qadr) as it pertains to the one who wrote it, Almighty God.

When I thought of the concept of Qadr, and how it tangled with the idea of free will, I had an enormous amount of trouble reconciling the two. When I would talk to people, they would give strange analogies. For example, A parent offers a child a choice between items even though the parent “knows” what their child wants. Or the “roller-coaster” analogy which places you on a roller-coaster and you merely observe the ride. Or the teacher analogy, who administers a test and from his knowledge, knows who of his student will pass and who will fail. These analogies all fall short, as they overlook key aspects of the relationship between God and man.

The nature of man is interesting. We are brought into this world with our environment and our faculties, and we use our language (a whole different rabbit-hole) to lasso and rein in the universe like metaphysical cowboys. We draw lines and make boxes, and inadvertently place ourselves within these boxes. The problem arises when we face things that do not fall neatly within our boxes. So what do we do? We make bigger boxes.

I am proposing here a bigger box.

We all grew up learning about the coordinate plane, spacetime, the like. This is known as Euclidean geometry.

Looks a little something like this.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/229/1*Vldyf6sjL_CZoRMjitx5Lg.png)

_2d plane_

You got your horizontal motion (x) and your vertical motion (y), and if you want to get real fancy, your depth motion (z) for 3D. 3D, that’s where we are, that’s how we see things. But is it how we perceive things? In our mind’s eye, can we fathom beyond the Euclidean?

Enter Hyperbolic geometry:

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/279/1*8OeXddca1r1-GjA5s5Dm2g.gif)

_hyperbolic fractal_

This trippy thing is an example of a hyperbolic fractal surface. Now what is interesting about the hyperbolic fractal surface is the fact that it has finite area but **infinite **perimeter.

If you were to traverse this space, you would find that you can cover an infinite distance and go to an infinite amount of novel locations, but only within the finite area of the circle. This is because every edge of the surface branches out to self similar but smaller and smaller edges.

You might be wondering at this point “Hey Yusuf, what does this have to do with Qadr”

Consider, for a moment, the majesty of Allah. When Allah commanded the pen to write, did He really “command” “a pen” “to write”, in our sense of the terms?

Your life, rather than being a screenplay and you’re acting out your part, is more like a video game. You have an **infinite **amount of choices that play out in the finite subspace called your life. What was written, given to you by Allah, does not interfere or contradict with what you can choose as a human.

At every choice and turn you have an infinite amount of options, but this subspace of options is constrained by your choice in the previous turn, in the previous Infinite Option Subspace.

The ending of the game is a result of your actions, either to do good or fall into evil.

As such, we are not judged based on the space we were given, but how we chose to navigate that space.

I hope this might help bring clarity to those who are also struggling to understand like I was, and I hope it might help put you on a path to find the inner peace you are seeking.
