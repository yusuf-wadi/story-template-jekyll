There seems to be a literary virus going around obsessed with efficiency.

Now don’t get me wrong, everyone loves some efficiency every once in a while, but there’s an argument to be made that efficiency for efficiency’s sake is robbing us of what makes us human.

Do less word make better sentence?

> No frills.

> No excess

> Pure. Meaning

Or long meaningless statements that convey nothing but neutrality and buzzwords.

> As we **delve deeper** into the current landscape, it’s clear that **synergy** is the **driving force** of success in today’s fast-paced business environment. We need to **harness** our collective potential to **optimize** our workflows and **empower** our teams to achieve maximum output

See what I mean? This is not how a human talks. A human should flow. Should say the wrong word and mean something new. Should fill out and pad a statement to convey more than what is stated. To carry a piece of them in their expression. To decorate their words and stamp it with their own seal of approval.

Is the future we are headed to that of sounding like an out of touch corporate ad or an unwanted email?

Now as an ML engineer by trade I can say with confidence that this is not a fault of the particular model architecture itself. The model is much like an impressionable child. The issue is that this impressionable child is being raised by nefarious and narcissistic parents. Corporations obsessed with squeezing humanity dry of all value and meaning. Automating and mechanizing more and more, until there’s no frontier left unscathed.

First it was labor, then the artisans, and now human speech itself.

What is next? Where left is there to turn?

Human language is where it gets really tricky. The pervasive nature of the cheapening of the human word down to tokens and probability. Its infectious. In the sense that we begin adopting some of the mannerisms and expressions of those we communicate with. And if the one we are communicating with is some aggregate of all human speech through a soul-crushing corporate filter, we bear the risk of being absorbed into the machine. Tricked into believing that this impressively quick-witted context engine speaks how we ought to speak. I mean hey, its working out for “him”.

This requires however the shedding of identity. [The shedding of the ability for your brain to create its own means of expression.](https://www.nctneurofeedback.com/post/the-default-mode-network-dmn-and-training-its-abilities-in-the-brain)

Once everything is outsourced, whats left of you?

We invent tools for a purpose.

Money for the exchange of goods and services

Brushes for the strokes of genius

Hammers for shaping and mending

Computers for the emulation of reality

Medicine for rejuvenation and healing

A human, a wielder of tools.

Would be a shame for the tool to wield you.
