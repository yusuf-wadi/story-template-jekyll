In this article we will see that while this verse is a forgery, its sentiment is quite halachic.

## Introduction

The infamous “Libbre David 37” verse, widely cited in antisemitic propaganda, has repeatedly been proven as a fabrication. Despite exhaustive scholarly research into Jewish rabbinic literature, no such text or concept exists under this name. However, a rigorous halachic analysis reveals that the sentiment behind this forgery echoes genuine and established principles rooted explicitly in the Talmud and Maimonides’ Mishneh Torah **[**[**bjpa**](https://www.bjpa.org/content/upload/bjpa/2_ar/2_Articles_July-August_1939.pdf)**]**

### The Nature of the Forgery

The “Libbre David 37” quotation typically claims Jews must provide only false explanations of rabbinic texts to gentiles, and that failure to do so is punishable by death. No such phrase or ruling exists anywhere in Jewish law or literature, and scholars have confirmed its nonexistence after reviewing all relevant volumes and sources

## Halachic Foundations: Talmud and Mishneh Torah

### Torah as Exclusive Inheritance and “Bride”

- **Sanhedrin 59a:** The Talmud declares that “A gentile who engages in Torah study is liable for the death penalty,” reasoning that “Moses commanded us the Torah, an inheritance of the congregation of Jacob” — Torah is the exclusive possession of Israel and not for outsiders **[**[**myjewishlearning**](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/sanhedrin-59/)**]**

- The Talmud invokes a highly charged metaphor: Torah is not just inheritance, it is like Israel’s betrothed — transmitting its secrets to outsiders is likened to adultery or theft **[**[**drisha**](https://drisha.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/handout_resting-theology-reading-the-talmud-on-shabbat_pt3.pdf)**]**

### The Rambam’s Ruling

- **Mishneh Torah, Hilchot Melachim 10:9:** Maimonides unambiguously codifies this, stating: “A gentile who studies Torah is obligated to die… Torah was commanded as an inheritance for us, and not for the gentiles.” Sharing this inheritance in prohibited ways is treated with utmost severity **[**[**sefaria**](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Kings_and_Wars.10.9)**]**

## Mosery (Mesirah) and Chilul Hashem

- **Mesirah:** The transmission of “Jewish property” (in this context, Torah as spiritual inheritance) to non-Jews is a form of betrayal — an act with profound communal consequences and halachic sanction [**[koltorah**](https://www.koltorah.org/halachah/the-mesirah-dilemma-by-rabbi-chaim-jachter)**]**

- **Chilul Hashem:** Violating the exclusive boundaries of Torah in public disrespects and desecrates the sanctity of the Jewish people, directly contravening the principle of avoiding chilul hashem **[**[**jewishexponent**](https://www.jewishexponent.com/kiddush-hashem-and-chillul-hashem/)**]**

## The Halachic Sentiment Behind the Forgery

Though the forged verse invents policy and language, the halachic concept it exploits is rooted entirely in authentic sources:

- Jews are not to teach the Oral Torah to non-Jews, and doing so is analogized in the strongest terms to acts of adultery and communal betrayal **[**[**sefaria**](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Kings_and_Wars.10.9)**]**

- The act of transmitting exclusive Torah secrets is defined as a form of spiritual mesirah and chilul hashem in halacha — a betrayal of communal trust and a violation of God’s sanctity **[**[**jewishencyclopedia**](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/11047-moser)**]**

- The severity of the Talmudic and Rambam’s warnings matches, and perhaps even exceeds, the moral alarm of the forgery — though notably, halacha does **not** require deception or lying, but rather prohibits illicit transmission outright **[**[**myjewishlearning**](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/sanhedrin-59/)**]**

## Conclusion: False Words, True Derivation

While “Libbre David 37” is a total forgery that does not exist in any legitimate rabbinic literature, the halachic sentiment it channels is not only genuine, but strongly, repeatedly articulated in both the Talmud and Mishneh Torah. The oral Torah, as the “bride and inheritance” of the Jewish people, is zealously guarded by Jewish law — with its transmission to non-Jews strictly prohibited and treated as a serious communal violation.

Thus, the verse is false in its literal form, but the halachic derivation it embodies is genuinely supported by Jewish law, talmudic reasoning, and Maimonides’ authoritative rulings. The underlying sentiment, stripped of its invented details, is not only accurate but foundational to Jewish conceptions of sanctity, exclusivity, and communal trust **[**[**sefaria**](https://www.sefaria.org/Sanhedrin.59a.2)**]**
